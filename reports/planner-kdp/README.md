## Key findings

1. The Planner niche had 1,165 books across Amazon Best Sellers and New Releases lists as of November 16, 2026. Estimated daily sales were 418, and the niche scored 62, ranking 373 of 1,715 niches.
2. Amazon search demand is strongest for wedding and dated planners. The keyword "wedding planner" recorded 51,028 searches, while "day planner 2027" recorded 12,426 searches during the data period.
3. The Planner niche has a relatively low review barrier. A total of 803 books have fewer than 10 reviews, and 85.7% of sampled books have fewer than 50 reviews. Only 1.4% of sampled books sell 100 or more estimated monthly units.
4. Independently published books account for 79.7% of the Planner niche. A total of 58.1% of sampled books were published in the last year, and the niche includes 129 breakouts.
5. Planner prices have a median of $10.99, with a $8.99 P25 and $16.99 P75. The median book has 122 pages, and 8.5x10.98 is the most frequently recorded trim size.

## How big is the niche

The Planner niche contains 1,165 books across Amazon Best Sellers and New Releases lists. This includes 937 Best Sellers entries and 357 New Releases entries. Print books account for 1,092 listings, compared with 73 Kindle eBooks. Estimated daily sales across the niche total 418 units.

The niche scores 62 out of 100 and ranks 373 of 1,715 niches. Its demand component scores 0.795, the review barrier scores 0.969 and the indie component scores 0.797. This places Planner in the upper portion of the measured niche market. The score covers a broad product category, however, rather than one clearly defined planner audience.

## Competition and the review barrier

Review counts suggest that publishers can enter the niche without competing exclusively against established listings. Of the 1,165 sampled books, 803 have fewer than 10 reviews, 195 have 10-50, 58 have 50-100, 72 have 100-500 and 37 have 500 or more. The sample median is 2 reviews, and 85.7% of books have fewer than 50 reviews. The niche-level median is 8 reviews. Many competing books therefore have relatively short review histories.

Most sampled books have estimated monthly sales below 30 units. A total of 1,086 books fall into that band, 58 into 30-100, 14 into 100-300, 2 into 300-1,000 and none into 1,000+. Only 1.4% of sampled books sell 100 or more units monthly. The niche-level indie share is 79.7%, compared with 74.5% in the sampled book data. Independently published and empty-publisher listings account for a substantial share of the competition. Recent releases make up 58.1% of the sample, showing a steady flow of new books into the niche.

## Should you publish in this niche?

Planner offers a viable broad niche for independent publishers, with a score of 62, estimated daily sales of 418 and an indie share of 79.7%. Its clearest advantage is the low review barrier. The sample median is 2 reviews, and 85.7% of sampled books have fewer than 50. Publishers can target defined audiences through sub-niches such as parents, seniors, students or nurses, which have scores between 61 and 66. A publisher with a differentiated product for a specific audience or practical task has the clearest route into the market.

Sales concentration is the main concern. Only 1.4% of sampled books sell 100 or more estimated units monthly, while 1,086 books sell fewer than 30. Generic planner concepts therefore enter a crowded field where most listings show limited sales. The leading books also cover different formats, audiences and price points. Publishers should assess keyword-level demand separately from the production costs and pricing of their chosen format. The available data does not establish a single seasonal peak or a universal launch window for the broad Planner niche.

## Method and limits

The niche's books are those on an Amazon.com Best Sellers or New Releases list in the three days to 2026-11-16 whose AI classification falls in this niche: 1,165 books. The review, sales and price distributions use the 1,165 of them KDPTop has full details for; sales are estimated from sales rank.

List data comes from KDPTop's daily crawl of every Amazon.com Books and Kindle Best Sellers and New Releases list; book details come from the list cards, book pages and Keepa; search volumes come from a third-party keyword tool and are estimates. Adult content categories are excluded.

Limits: lists reflect rank, not actual sales, and every sales figure is estimated from rank; books KDPTop has not seen are not counted.
