## Key findings

1. Amazon.com recorded 118,144 estimated searches for kids coloring books in October 2026, followed by 2,658 purchases. Search volume fell 28.13% month over month, with demand reaching its highest levels in November and December.
2. The 200 selling kids coloring books had a median of 169 reviews. A total of 22.5% had fewer than 50 reviews, and independently published books accounted for 61.0% of the selling list.
3. The median price of selling kids coloring books was $9.76, with the middle half priced between $6.99 and $9.99. The median book length was 102 pages.
4. The leading kids coloring books included Cozy Corner: Coloring Book for Adults and Kids (Cozy Spaces Coloring), with estimated monthly sales of 15,660 units, and Cozy Cuties: Coloring Book for Adults and Kids (Cozy Spaces Coloring), with 7,230.
5. The related keyword kids coloring books ages 8-12 recorded 4,256 searches and 5,227 competing products. Popular kids coloring books had 16,311 searches and 1,279 products.

## How many people search for "kids coloring books" on Amazon?

Amazon.com recorded 118,144 estimated searches for kids coloring books in October 2026, followed by 2,658 purchases at a 2.2% purchase rate. Search volume fell 28.13% month over month. Demand peaks in November and December, making the final months of the year the strongest seasonal period in the available data. ## How hard is it to compete for "kids coloring books"?

The 200 selling books had a median of 169 reviews, while 22.5% had fewer than 50 reviews. The first print-book search page contained 10 organic books, with a median of 2,117 reviews and no independently published titles. Its estimated monthly revenue was $317,614. The Kindle eBook page also contained 10 organic books, but had a median of 25 reviews and an independently published share of 44.4%. Across the selling list, independently published books accounted for 61.0%, and 22.5% had been published in the last year. An estimated 46.5% sell at least 100 copies per month. The difference between the print and Kindle search-page review medians shows why publishers need to assess competition by format.

The most frequent stated promises are easy or for beginners at 36.0%, relaxing at 30.0%, and bold lines at 18.5%. Gift wording appears in 13.5% of titles. ## Should you publish a "kids coloring books" book?

The data supports entering kids coloring books with a defined audience or subject. A generic title would need a clear point of differentiation to compete. Independently published books account for 61.0% of the selling list, and 46.5% of those books sell an estimated 100 or more copies monthly. Independent publishers are active in this niche, particularly with focused concepts such as preschool cutting activities, age-specific coloring and cozy themes.

The main challenge is the strength of established print-book results. The first print search page has a median of 2,117 reviews and contains no independently published titles. Search volume also fell 28.13% month over month in October 2026. Publishers should factor in the November and December demand peak, the $9.76 median selling price and the limited value of the supplied category data when assessing a new title.

## Method and limits

Search volume, purchases and the monthly change are estimates for 2026-10. The selling books are those KDPTop saw in the last 30 days whose title has every word of "kids coloring books" (any order, singular or plural): 147,275 books. Reviews, sales, prices and title wording are counted on the 200 best sellers among those with a sales estimate, so the figures describe the top a new book stands next to, not every book of the kind. Sales are estimated from sales rank.

Title length, the counts a title states ("100 puzzles") and promise words are read off the titles by rule: they say what the title says, and nobody checked the book holds that many. "Title built as", "listing promises", "used for" and "likely buyer" are a language model's choices from fixed lists after reading 177 of the listings; the last is a guess.

The first search page is one reading of the page by KDPTop. Sponsored cards change with the time, the shopper and the delivery location, so their share is a sample of that one reading.

List data comes from KDPTop's daily crawl of every Amazon.com Books and Kindle Best Sellers and New Releases list; book details come from the list cards, book pages and sales-rank history; search volumes are estimates. Adult content categories are excluded.

Limits: lists reflect rank, not actual sales, and every sales figure is estimated from rank; books KDPTop has not seen are not counted.
