# Amazon KDP Week 46: 28,346 New Best Sellers

Amazon KDP recorded 28,346 new Best Sellers in Week 46, including 16,600 indie titles. See the leading categories, prices, breakout books and publishing opportunities.

## Key takeaways

- Indie and KDP publishers recorded 16,600 breakouts, ahead of the 10,047 attributed to traditional publishers.
- Nonfiction led all top-level categories with 5,837 breakouts, making it the first category worth investigating for practical and instructional titles.
- The median review count at breakout was zero, so an established review history was not necessary for every new bestseller.
- The median breakout price was $11.95, but both lower-priced and $20+ books recorded substantial breakout activity.
- Monitor category-level results in Week 47, but do not confuse breakout activity with sustained sales or Movers & Shakers rank gains.

Amazon KDP recorded 28,346 new books reaching Best Sellers lists during 2026-W46, covering November 3–9. Independently published books accounted for 16,600 of those breakouts. That puts indie publishing ahead of traditionally published books for the week, with Nonfiction leading all categories by a considerable margin.

The other useful signal is reader-review activity. The median review count at breakout was zero. A new book did not need an established review history to reach a Best Sellers list during this period. For publishers planning their next release, that makes category selection, positioning and launch execution worth examining before spending months building an audience.

## 28,346 books reached Best Sellers lists in one week

The week produced 28,346 breakouts across Amazon KDP categories. November 5 was the busiest day, with 5,513 new books reaching Best Sellers lists. November 8 followed with 4,773. November 3 recorded 2,598, while November 4 recorded 2,955. November 6 and November 7 contributed 3,539 and 3,198 respectively.

The daily chart reports zero for November 9. That figure needs some caution. It should not be interpreted as evidence that no books broke out on that date, particularly when the reporting period itself ends on November 9. Publishers should avoid using that single daily figure to draw conclusions about demand or publishing activity.

A breakout has a specific meaning here: a new book appearing on a New Releases list within the last 60 days that also reached a Best Sellers list. This is a useful measure of recent publishing activity, not a count of all new Amazon releases.

For an indie publisher, the November 5 peak is a reminder that breakout activity can vary considerably from day to day. A launch that reaches a category bestseller position is competing in a changing field. Checking rankings regularly gives a more useful picture than looking at a single snapshot.

## Nonfiction leads, with Religion & Spirituality in second place

Nonfiction recorded 5,837 breakouts, making it the largest category in this week's report. Religion & Spirituality followed with 2,319. The gap is substantial. Literature & Fiction recorded 1,487, Foreign Languages reached 1,463, and Children's Books came in at 1,457.

The next group was considerably smaller. Computers & Technology recorded 981 breakouts, Travel had 960, and Business & Money reached 958. These categories still produced meaningful new-release activity, but they did not approach Nonfiction's volume.

For publishers choosing a category, volume is only one part of the decision. A large category offers evidence of substantial publishing activity, but it also means more competing books. A smaller category may be easier to target, although fewer breakouts do not automatically mean better sales potential.

Nonfiction deserves attention from publishers working on practical guides, reference material and instructional books. The appearance of WILDER PRESS's The Essential Lost Book of Independence: 250+ Illustrated Skills for Self-Sufficiency, Off-Grid Living, Food Preservation, Natural Remedies & Emergency Preparedness From Beginner to Expert in Garden Design also shows how a broad subject can be positioned for a more specific category. Its category position is worth studying, but one book cannot establish a repeatable publishing formula.

Children's Books and Foreign Languages are also worth monitoring. Their breakout totals were close to each other. Publishers considering kids' books or language-learning titles have evidence of new-release activity in both areas, though this report does not separate individual subcategories or book formats.

## Indie publishers account for 16,600 breakouts

Independently published books accounted for 16,600 breakouts during Week 46. Traditionally published books contributed 10,047, while 1,699 were unclassified. KDP and indie titles therefore represented the largest publishing group in the report.

This is a useful result for self-publishers. New indie books were not simply appearing in the margins of Amazon's bestseller categories. They represented a substantial share of the books reaching those lists. That gives publishers a reason to keep studying new releases rather than concentrating exclusively on established titles from large publishing houses.

The individual books provide some context. Butterfly 6 : Phase 2 "Chrysalis" by Ashley Antoinette reached rank 1 in Black & African American. Its reported BSR was 184, with 9,930 estimated monthly units and $128,991 in estimated monthly revenue. It had 302 reviews and a 4.9 rating.

Black American Denim by Evelyn Latrice also reached rank 1 in Black & African American. It had a reported BSR of 257, 13 reviews and a 4.9 rating. Its estimated monthly units were 4,860, with estimated monthly revenue of $63,131. Both titles are independently published fiction novels.

The difference in review counts is worth noticing. Black American Denim had only 13 reviews in the supplied data, yet its reported category position was the same as Butterfly 6 : Phase 2 "Chrysalis". That does not mean reviews are irrelevant. It does show that a substantial review history was not a requirement for every book in this week's breakout examples.

These figures are estimates derived from sales rank, not sales or revenue figures reported by Amazon. BSR is Amazon Best Sellers Rank, where a lower number indicates a better position. Estimated monthly units and revenue should be treated as directional evidence rather than verified financial results.

## The middle price bands contain the most breakouts

The median breakout price was $11.95. The largest individual price band was $5–9.99, with 6,677 books. Titles priced below $5 recorded 6,434 breakouts. The $10–14.99 band contributed 5,486, while $20+ recorded 5,679. The $15–19.99 range had the smallest total, at 3,746.

The distribution does not point to a single winning price. Lower-priced books were active, but books priced above $20 also reached Best Sellers lists in meaningful numbers. The median price of $11.95 gives publishers a useful reference point, not a mandatory price target.

For low-content publishers, coloring-book creators and authors of children's books, the practical question is what the reader receives at each price. A higher price needs a clear reason, such as substantial content, useful illustrations, a specialized subject or a format that justifies the difference. A lower price can make a book easier to try, but it does not guarantee stronger sales.

The examples reinforce the range. Butterfly 6 : Phase 2 "Chrysalis" and Black American Denim were both priced at $12.99. WILDER PRESS's The Essential Lost Book of Independence was priced at $7.86, while David L. Owens's Lost Timeless Skills of Independence: 180 Illustrated Old-Fashioned Skills and 48 Hands-On Projects for Food Preservation, Homesteading, Home Repairs, Money-Saving and Off-Grid Living was priced at $9.99.

Those nonfiction examples share an independence and practical-skills theme, but their prices and category placements differ. Publishers can use them as research starting points. They should not assume that copying a title structure or price will reproduce the same result.

## Zero reviews at the median changes the launch calculation

One of the more useful figures in the report is the median review count at breakout: zero. In practical terms, at least half of the books represented by this median had no reviews at the point they reached a Best Sellers list.

That matters to new publishers who assume a book needs dozens of reviews before it can compete. The Week 46 data does not support that assumption. A new title can reach a bestseller category without an established review history. However, that is not proof that reviews have no influence on conversion or long-term performance.

Black American Denim offers a concrete example. Evelyn Latrice's novel reached rank 1 in Black & African American with 13 reviews. Its 4.9 rating is encouraging, but the review count is still relatively modest compared with Butterfly 6 : Phase 2 "Chrysalis", which had 302 reviews.

The distinction between reaching a list and maintaining a position matters. A breakout measures entry into a Best Sellers list. It does not tell us how long the book stayed there, how many readers bought it after the reporting period or whether its ranking continued to improve.

For a new KDP release, the sensible approach is to publish a complete, properly presented book, select relevant categories and keywords, and monitor the ranking after launch. Reviews should be earned through genuine reader experiences. The data gives no reason to treat artificial review-building as a publishing strategy.

## What publishers should watch in Week 47

The first area to watch is Nonfiction. Its 5,837 breakouts made it the strongest top-level category in Week 46. Publishers working on practical guides, home reference books, self-sufficiency and instructional content should examine the specific categories where new titles are gaining positions. The two independence-themed books in this report offer useful examples of subject positioning.

The second is Literature & Fiction, alongside the Black & African American category represented by Ashley Antoinette and Evelyn Latrice. Both novels reached rank 1 in their category. Their estimated monthly revenue figures suggest meaningful commercial activity, although the estimates are not verified earnings and should not be used as guaranteed income projections.

Third, keep an eye on Children's Books and Foreign Languages. Their breakout totals were nearly level. For publishers developing coloring books, kids' activity books or language-learning material, the next research step is to examine narrower categories and competing book listings. The current figures do not identify which individual formats or keywords generated the most sales.

There is also a limitation concerning Movers & Shakers. This week's supplied data identifies breakout activity, category positions and estimated monthly performance, but does not provide before-and-after Movers & Shakers rankings. It is therefore not possible to name the biggest rank gains or claim that a particular book had the largest weekly jump. A breakout is not the same measurement as a Movers & Shakers gain.

For Week 47, the sensible watchlist is category-level activity, new indie entries, pricing and the review counts of books reaching bestseller positions. Look for repeated patterns across several releases rather than building an entire publishing plan around one successful title. The strongest opportunity is not necessarily the category with the most books. It is the category where a publisher can produce a relevant book and compete on its actual content.
