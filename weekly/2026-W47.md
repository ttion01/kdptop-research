# Amazon KDP Week 47: 24,353 New Best Sellers Break Through

Amazon KDP recorded 24,353 new Best Sellers breakouts in Week 47. Indie books accounted for 14,702, with Nonfiction leading category activity.

## Key takeaways

- KDP and other indie publishers accounted for 14,702 of 24,353 reported breakouts, approximately 60.4% of the weekly total.
- Nonfiction led category activity with 2,867 breakouts, followed by Religion & Spirituality with 1,579.
- The $5–9.99 price range had the most breakouts at 5,972, while the overall median price was $11.06.
- Would You Rather in Finland offers a useful example of combining an established activity-book format with a specific geographic keyword angle.
- The median review count at breakout was zero, but the supplied data does not include Movers & Shakers rank changes or enough information to measure keyword-level competition.

Amazon recorded 24,353 new books reaching Best Sellers lists during November 10–16, 2026. Independently published KDP books accounted for 14,702 of those breakouts, or about 60.4%. Traditional publishers contributed 8,900, while 751 books had unclassified publishing status. Indie publishers captured the larger share of new category entries this week.

## November 12 was the busiest breakout day

November 12 recorded the week's highest daily total, with 4,965 new books reaching Best Sellers lists. That was considerably higher than November 10, which recorded 2,353, and November 11, which recorded 2,586. The increase on November 12 suggests a concentrated wave of new releases or category movement rather than an even distribution throughout the week.

November 15 was another active day, with 4,294 breakouts. November 13 recorded 2,981, while November 14 had 2,694. The dataset reports zero for November 16, the final day of the reporting period. That figure needs caution because it may reflect an incomplete reporting window rather than an absence of new entries.

There is also a discrepancy worth flagging. The six daily figures add up to 19,873, which is 4,480 below the reported weekly total of 24,353. The daily chart therefore does not fully account for the headline figure. Publishers should use the weekly total for the overall market comparison and treat the daily breakdown as a partial view.

For release planning, November 12 is the date to watch in this dataset. A single busy day does not establish a recurring launch pattern, but it shows how quickly category competition can change during a week. Publishers tracking new releases should check their target categories regularly instead of relying on one weekly rank snapshot.

## Nonfiction led the category count with 2,867 breakouts

Nonfiction had the most new Best Sellers entries, recording 2,867 breakouts. Religion & Spirituality followed with 1,579, while Computers & Technology reached 1,448. Foreign Languages recorded 1,405, and Literature & Fiction had 1,375.

Children's Books also had substantial activity, with 1,318 breakouts. Travel recorded 972, and Business & Money reached 820. These figures count new books entering Best Sellers lists, not total category sales or the number of books available for purchase.

The spread gives publishers a useful starting point for category research. Nonfiction had considerably more breakout activity than any other listed category. That means publishers considering nonfiction have a large pool of potential buyers and competing new titles to investigate. A broad category count alone cannot establish how much room remains for another book.

The other categories offer different research directions. Computers & Technology and Foreign Languages both recorded more than 1,400 entries. Children's Books had 1,318, making it a meaningful category to investigate for illustrated books, educational titles and other children's publishing projects. Those are possible product directions, not proof that every subcategory has enough demand.

For a new project, drill down from the top-level category into the actual Amazon subcategories and examine the books holding strong ranks. A broad category can contain several smaller markets with very different competition levels. The breakout figures identify where new books are appearing; they do not tell you which individual keywords are easiest to rank for.

## KDP publishers supplied 60.4% of the week's breakouts

Independently published books accounted for 14,702 of the 24,353 reported breakouts. Traditional publishers contributed 8,900, and 751 entries remained unclassified. KDP's share works out to approximately 60.4% of the weekly total.

That is a substantial number of indie titles reaching category bestseller lists. It also gives publishers a useful reality check: independent books are competing successfully for visibility across the categories tracked in this report. The numbers cover more than one publishing format, so they should not be read as a count of low-content books or coloring books alone.

One example is Escaping Gravity 3 by Bruce Sentar (ASIN B0HFYD9VLR). The independently published fiction novel reached number one in Superheroes. Its reported BSR was 1,508, with 720 estimated monthly units and $3,593 in estimated monthly revenue. It had 211 reviews and a 4.9 rating.

Vaccine Informed: What Parents Should Know About Disease Risk, Vaccine Benefit, and Safety by Brittany Todd (B0HQ9CW79G) also reached number one in its category, Immune Systems. The KDP title had a reported BSR of 17,735, 11 reviews and estimated monthly sales of 132 units. Its estimated monthly revenue was $1,715.

These examples show that indie breakouts can come from very different kinds of books. They also show why category position and overall sales rank need separate attention. A number-one position in a narrower category does not necessarily mean a book has a strong overall Amazon rank. Check both before using a bestseller listing as evidence for a publishing opportunity.

## The $5–9.99 price range had the most entries

The $5–9.99 price band recorded 5,972 breakouts, the highest count among the five reported price ranges. Books priced below $5 contributed 5,398. The $10–14.99 range had 4,662, while $15–19.99 recorded 3,163. Books priced at $20 or more accounted for 4,825 breakouts.

The median price across the week's breakouts was $11.06. That puts the middle of the market slightly above the $10–14.99 band, even though the $5–9.99 range had the largest individual count.

For publishers, the lower price bands deserve attention because they contain many of the books entering bestseller lists. A $4.99 fiction title such as Escaping Gravity 3 shows one way a book can compete at a relatively low price. However, its reported monthly revenue estimate of $3,593 also shows why price alone cannot explain performance. Category placement, reader interest and the existing audience matter.

The higher price ranges are worth examining too. There were 4,825 breakouts at $20 or above, more than the number in the $15–19.99 band. Publishers working on cookbooks, illustrated books or substantial nonfiction should not assume that a lower list price is necessary to reach a Best Sellers list.

These are breakout counts, not conversion rates. The chart does not show how many books were published at each price or how many failed to reach bestseller status. It therefore cannot establish that a particular price point gives a new book a better chance of succeeding. Compare competing books with similar page counts, formats and subject matter before setting a price.

## Books worth examining from this week's category leaders

Scott Adams: A Life of Persuasion and Principle by Joel Pollak (B0HRCNCBG1) reached number one in Campaigns & Elections. Its reported BSR was 182, with 5,145 estimated monthly units and $51,399 in estimated monthly revenue. Published November 12, the biography had five reviews and a 5.0 rating. Its $9.99 price makes it a useful example of a current-affairs biography attracting substantial estimated activity with a relatively small review count.

STEEL-TOED LEADERSHIP FROM THE CORPS TO CORPORATE: What the Marine Corps Teaches Every Leader That Business School Never Will by Will Quinn (B0HKDMGLHJ) reached number one in Distribution & Warehouse Management. It had a BSR of 3,352 and 540 estimated monthly units, with estimated monthly revenue of $5,395. Its $9.99 price gives nonfiction publishers another example of a focused professional subject reaching the top of a specific category.

Two other titles illustrate the range of category competition. The Heist Before Christmas by Lucy Score (B0H6R3KYY7) reached number one in Mystery with a BSR of 22. It had 390 reviews, a 4.7 rating and estimated monthly revenue of $2,156. Meanwhile, Would You Rather in Finland: Explore the Land of Sauna, Sisu and Santa With 254 Fun and Thought-Provoking Questions by Epic Journey Press (B0HPCLPHL7) reached number one in Finland. This KDP game book was priced at $2.99 and had 930 estimated monthly units.

The Finland title is particularly relevant to publishers considering activity books and low-content-adjacent formats. It combines a familiar question-game structure with a specific geographic subject. That gives the product a defined angle for keyword and audience research. Its reported BSR of 206,766 is also a reminder that category leadership can coexist with a much weaker overall rank.

Another example is 101 Mistakes Humans Make to Mess Up to Mess Up Their Dogs by Dina Zaphiris (B0HQZCB2GJ). The book reached number one in Care & Health, with a BSR of 7,599 and 180 estimated monthly units. Its $17.09 price and estimated monthly revenue of $3,076 illustrate how a focused pet-care subject can occupy a different price position from inexpensive activity books.

## What publishers should watch in Week 48

The clearest research priorities for the coming week are Nonfiction, Religion & Spirituality, Computers & Technology and Foreign Languages. These categories had substantial breakout activity, giving publishers plenty of new listings to examine. The next step is to identify the subcategories where newer indie books are gaining ground without facing an overwhelming number of established titles.

Low-content and activity-book publishers should examine the structure of Would You Rather in Finland. Its geographic angle provides a concrete keyword-research direction: a familiar game format combined with a specific destination. Similar concepts need their own demand checks. A successful title does not establish that every country, city or travel theme has enough buyers.

Coloring-book publishers and children's-book creators should keep Children's Books on their research list. The category recorded 1,318 breakouts. That figure supports further investigation, but the available examples do not provide individual coloring-book sales or keyword performance. Check competing interiors, age positioning, cover presentation and review history before committing to a series.

There is no Movers & Shakers rank-change dataset in this week's figures. That means the biggest gains in that list cannot be identified or ranked reliably from the supplied information. Publishers should not confuse a new Best Sellers entry with a Movers & Shakers gain. They measure different types of movement.

The review count at breakout had a median of zero. That is useful evidence for publishers assessing how much review history a new entrant may need. A new book can reach a bestseller list without an established review count. It does not mean reviews are irrelevant to long-term sales or that a new title will automatically rank with no reviews.

For Week 48, track the same subcategories and compare the new entrants against their previous ranks. Watch for repeat appearances, sustained overall BSR improvements and titles that maintain activity after their initial release period. Those signals will provide a better basis for choosing a publishing project than a single category bestseller position.
