# Amazon KDP Breakouts in 2026-W48: 20,773 New Books

Amazon KDP recorded 20,773 new Best Sellers in 2026-W48, including 12,317 indie books. See leading categories, price ranges and breakout titles.

## Key takeaways

- Nonfiction produced 4,157 breakouts, making it the first top-level category worth investigating, although its broad total does not identify the strongest subcategories.
- Indie publishers accounted for 12,317 of 20,773 breakouts, approximately 59% of the weekly total.
- The $5-$9.99 price range led with 5,182 breakouts, while the overall median price was $10.99.
- The median review count at breakout was zero, so new publishers should investigate category fit and launch timing rather than treating an established review history as essential.
- Track Christmas romance and interactive seasonal products alongside specialist nonfiction, but use narrower category data before committing production costs.

Amazon recorded 20,773 new books reaching Best Sellers lists during November 17-23, 2026. Independently published books accounted for 12,317 of those breakouts, or roughly 59% of the weekly total. That gives indie publishers a substantial share of new category entries, even in a week when traditional publishers contributed 7,392 titles.

Nonfiction led all top-level categories with 4,157 breakouts. The median review count at breakout was zero, and the median book price was $10.99. For publishers planning their next release, those two figures deserve attention: new books can reach category bestseller lists without an established review history, while the middle of the market remains close to the $10 price point. The figures below use estimated sales derived from Amazon Best Sellers Rank (BSR), not sales reported by Amazon.

## November 19 was the busiest day for new Best Sellers

November 19 produced 4,487 new books reaching Best Sellers lists, the highest daily total of the week. November 20 followed with 3,334, while November 22 recorded 3,792. The slowest day was November 17, with 330 breakouts. The daily figures show a concentrated surge in the middle of the reporting period, followed by continued activity through November 23.

The weekly total was 20,773. November 19 alone contributed more than one-fifth of all recorded breakouts. For publishers watching category competition, the daily pattern matters because bestseller entry is a moving target. A category with a relatively quiet day can receive a large batch of new competitors shortly afterward.

A breakout has a specific meaning here: a new book appearing on a New Releases list within the previous 60 days that reached a Best Sellers list. These numbers therefore track new-release entries rather than every book already selling well. They provide a useful view of new competition, although they do not show how long each title stayed on its list.

For a planned launch, check category activity across several days rather than relying on one snapshot. A category that looks manageable on Monday may have considerably more new entrants by Thursday. The November 19 spike is a good reason to keep monitoring rankings during launch week.

## Nonfiction generated more than 4,000 breakouts

Nonfiction led the category rankings with 4,157 breakouts. That was more than three times the 1,354 recorded in Religion & Spirituality, the second-place category. Foreign Languages followed with 1,259, then Literature & Fiction with 1,161 and Children's Books with 1,008.

Computers & Technology contributed 750 breakouts, Children's eBooks recorded 728, and Business & Money reached 689. The gap between Nonfiction and the other categories is substantial. It suggests that publishers looking for broad category activity should start their research there, but a large number of breakouts also means a larger pool of competing new releases.

These are top-level category totals. They do not identify which individual nonfiction subcategories produced the most successful launches. A cookbook, a medical reference title and a general-interest nonfiction book can have very different audiences and competitive conditions. The category total alone cannot tell a publisher which of those formats has the best commercial prospects.

The same caution applies to Children's Books and Children's eBooks. Their separate counts are 1,008 and 728. Publishers should examine the specific format and subcategory before treating them as one market. For a coloring book or children's activity book, a narrower category analysis will be more useful than the broad category ranking.

The practical reading is straightforward: Nonfiction has the most new-release activity, while Religion & Spirituality and Foreign Languages offer substantial secondary pools to investigate. Compare the opportunity score, current competing titles and estimated sales within the actual subcategory before committing to a manuscript.

## Indie publishers accounted for 59% of breakouts

Independently published books produced 12,317 of the 20,773 breakouts. Traditional publishers contributed 7,392, while 1,064 titles remained unclassified. Indie books therefore represented approximately 59% of the weekly total, compared with roughly 36% for traditional publishers.

That is a meaningful result for KDP publishers. More than half of the new books entering Best Sellers lists were independently published. Traditional publishers still generated a substantial number of entries, but indie releases had the larger share during this reporting week.

The unclassified group matters when interpreting the split. Those 1,064 books have no confirmed classification in this dataset, so they should not automatically be assigned to either publishing group. The indie share is calculated from the classified and unclassified weekly total, rather than assuming every uncertain title belongs to a particular publisher type.

The figures also do not establish that every indie breakout was profitable. A book can reach a category Best Sellers list with modest sales, depending on the category and competing titles. Entry into a list is a visibility signal, not a complete business result.

For publishers deciding what to release next, the indie share supports continued testing of focused KDP titles. It is particularly relevant to publishers who can produce useful reference books, fiction in established series or seasonal puzzle titles. The strongest decision still comes from checking the economics of the individual category, including pricing, production costs and the sales volume needed to recover those costs.

## The $5-$9.99 price range had the most breakouts

The $5-$9.99 price band recorded 5,182 breakouts, the highest count of any price range. Books priced below $5 contributed 4,794. Together, those two ranges accounted for 9,976 new Best Sellers entries, close to half the weekly total.

The $10-$14.99 range produced 3,873 breakouts, followed by $20 and above with 3,918. The $15-$19.99 band recorded 2,588, the smallest group. The median price across all breakouts was $10.99, putting the middle of the market just above the most active price band.

This is useful information for setting an initial price, particularly for fiction and shorter KDP titles. The $5-$9.99 range had the largest number of new entries, but that does not prove that a book priced there will sell more copies than a comparable title at $10.99. The chart counts books, not the revenue or conversion rate of each price band.

Higher-priced books also deserve attention. Behind the Knife - ABSITE Podcast Companion: 4th Edition, 2027 by Behind the Knife BTK was priced at $56.99. Its estimated monthly units were 103, with estimated monthly revenue of $5,870. Willis O'Brien - Lost Worlds, Giant Apes and Dinosaurs: From H.G. Wells to Mad Mad World by Greg Kulon was priced at $80.33, with estimated monthly units of 57 and revenue of $4,579.

These examples show why price comparisons need to include the product itself. A specialist reference book can support a much higher price than a short romance novel. Publishers should compare books with similar content, audience and production requirements rather than copying the most common price in the overall breakout dataset.

## Eight breakout books show different routes to category rankings

The leading examples cover several different publishing models. Ceveyn Nights (The Laurent Boys Book 2) by S. Yvonne reached number one in Romance. Its listed price was $9.99, its BSR was 540, and its estimated monthly sales were 2,580 units, producing estimated monthly revenue of $25,774. The book had 43 reviews and a 4.5 rating.

Unintended Cultivator: Volume Ten by Eric Dontigney reached number one in Myths & Legends. It had a BSR of 80, 346 reviews and a 4.8 rating. Estimated monthly sales were 2,777 units, with estimated monthly revenue of $16,634. Its $5.99 price puts it in the week's busiest price band. This is a useful example for series publishers: an established series can bring readers into a new volume, although the data does not isolate how much of its performance came from earlier books.

What Holly Heard: A Holiday Overheard Betrayal Grovel Romance (Words Of Ruin Book 4) by Sasha Rivers reached number one in Love Triangle. At $3.99, it had estimated monthly sales of 3,518 units and estimated monthly revenue of $14,037. Its 84 reviews and 4.2 rating provide some reader feedback, but its performance cannot be separated from the seasonal romance audience or the existing series readership.

KDP titles also appeared in specialist and seasonal categories. The Winter Hollow Murder – Advent Calendar Murder Mystery: A 24-Day Interactive Christmas Whodunit with Puzzles, Riddles & Clues for Mystery Lovers by Nora Everwood reached number one in Mystery. Priced at $18.91, it had 190 reviews, a 5.0 rating and estimated monthly sales of 298 units. Its interactive Advent calendar format gives it a clear seasonal use case.

Not So Merry After All: A Betrayal & Grovel Christmas Romance by Harley Chen reached number one in Later in Life. It was priced at $3.91, with a BSR of 311 and estimated monthly sales of 1,110 units. Its 68 reviews and 3.8 rating show that a perfect rating is not a requirement for a category-leading position.

Other examples include Behind the Knife - ABSITE Podcast Companion: 4th Edition, 2027, a specialist reference title, and Willis O'Brien - Lost Worlds, Giant Apes and Dinosaurs: From H.G. Wells to Mad Mad World, a high-priced biography. Ghosts In The Blood: The Extraordinary History of Chronic Lymphocytic Leukemia by Jeff Sharman MD also reached number one in Hematology, with a $26.59 price and estimated monthly revenue of $4,121.

The commercial lesson is that category fit and audience matter. The romance examples have much higher estimated unit volumes, while specialist books can generate meaningful estimated revenue from fewer units. These monthly figures are modelled from BSR and should be treated as estimates, not verified sales or royalty statements.

## What KDP publishers should watch next week

The next reporting period deserves close attention to seasonal books. The Winter Hollow Murder and the two Christmas romance titles demonstrate that holiday-focused products can reach category-leading positions during this period. Publishers considering a seasonal release should check how much time remains for production, listing preparation and customer delivery before choosing a format.

Nonfiction also deserves continued monitoring. Its 4,157 breakouts made it the largest category by a wide margin, but the total is too broad to justify publishing a generic nonfiction title. A specialist subject with a defined reader need is a more useful starting point. Behind the Knife - ABSITE Podcast Companion: 4th Edition, 2027 and Ghosts In The Blood illustrate the range of specialist subjects represented among the leading books.

For low-content publishers, this week's data offers less direct evidence. There are no separate breakout totals for blank journals, planners, logbooks or coloring books. Children's Books recorded 1,008 breakouts, but that number does not reveal how many were coloring or activity books. Publishers should avoid treating the overall children's category as proof of demand for a particular low-content format.

One additional figure is worth watching: the median review count at breakout was zero. New releases can reach Best Sellers lists before accumulating reviews. That gives new publishers a reason to pay attention to category selection, listing relevance and launch timing instead of assuming a large review base is a prerequisite.

There is no Movers & Shakers before-and-after rank data in this week's figures, so the biggest rank gains cannot be measured reliably here. The breakout counts identify new arrivals, not the titles that climbed the most positions in a short period. For next week, track daily category entries, prices and individual BSR changes separately. That will give publishers a better basis for deciding which niches deserve another round of research.

### Which Amazon KDP category had the most new Best Sellers?

### Can a new KDP book become a bestseller without reviews?

Yes. The median review count at breakout was zero in 2026-W48, meaning at least half of the recorded breakout books had no more than zero reviews at the point represented by this dataset. A category bestseller position does not guarantee sustained sales, and estimated units and revenue are modelled from BSR rather than reported by Amazon.
